## How Google Search Works

Google's search model operates on a simple principle: index billions of web pages, rank them by relevance and authority, and present ten blue links for the user to choose from. The user decides which result best answers their query.

The process involves three core steps:

1. **Crawling** — Google's bots discover pages by following links across the web.
2. **Indexing** — Pages are stored in Google's massive database, categorized by content type and topic.
3. **Ranking** — An algorithm evaluates hundreds of factors (keywords, backlinks, page speed, mobile-friendness) to determine which pages appear first.

For healthcare, this has meant that practices invest in SEO — optimizing their website for Google's ranking factors — to appear when patients search for terms like "dermatologist near me" or "best orthopedic surgeon in Austin."

## How AI Search Works

AI search engines operate on a fundamentally different model. Instead of ranking pages, they read, synthesize, and generate a single direct answer. There is no list of links to browse. The user receives one response — and makes their decision based on that alone.

The process involves:

1. **Retrieval** — The AI pulls information from multiple sources: websites, directories, databases, and knowledge graphs.
2. **Synthesis** — It reads and interprets the information, cross-referencing facts across sources.
3. **Generation** — It produces a coherent, natural-language answer, often citing sources.

When a patient asks an AI tool "who's the best orthopedic surgeon in Austin?", the AI doesn't scroll through Google results. It pulls from structured data, directory listings, your website's content, and third-party mentions to construct an answer.

## The Critical Difference for Healthcare

The shift from Google to AI search has outsized implications for healthcare because of how patients make medical decisions:

- **High-stakes queries** — Patients searching for healthcare providers are making important decisions. They want a clear, trustworthy answer — which is exactly what AI provides.
- **Single-answer format** — Unlike Google, where a patient might click through three or four results, AI gives one answer. Being second in AI search is being invisible.
- **Trust verification** — AI systems build trust by cross-referencing multiple authoritative sources. If your practice's data is inconsistent or incomplete, AI won't recommend you.

Consider this: a multi-location dermatology practice could rank #1 on Google for "dermatologist in [city]" by optimizing their website and running ads. But when someone asks an AI "which dermatologist should I see for acne?", the AI needs to:

1. Identify the practice as a dermatology provider
2. Associate it with the correct locations
3. Verify it through multiple authoritative sources
4. Extract relevant information (specialties, services, availability)

If any of these links break, the practice becomes invisible to AI search — regardless of its Google ranking.

## What AI Pulls From to Build Answers

AI search engines construct answers from four primary data sources:

| Source | What AI Extracts | Why It Matters |
|--------|------------------|----------------|
| **Structured data** | Schema markup on websites (type of practice, services, locations) | Tells AI exactly what your practice is |
| **Directory listings** | Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Google Business Profile profiles | Verifies your practice exists and is active |
| **Website content** | Headings, text, metadata, service pages | Provides detailed information about what you offer |
| **Third-party mentions** | News articles, reviews, professional directories | Builds authority and trust signals |

If your practice's information is scattered, inconsistent, or missing from these sources, AI won't find you. It'll recommend the practice whose data is cleaner and more complete.

## How Google AI Overviews Work for Healthcare

Google AI Overviews (formerly SGE) are Google's AI-generated summaries that appear at the top of search results. They synthesize information from multiple sources to provide a direct answer — and they're increasingly prominent for healthcare queries.

### How Google Selects Sources for AI Overviews

Google's AI Overview algorithm evaluates sources based on four criteria:

**1. Topical Relevance.** Does your content directly answer the specific question being asked? A general "about us" page won't be cited. A page titled "IVF Success Rates at [Clinic Name]: What to Expect" will.

**2. Source Authority.** Does Google trust your source? Verified Google Business Profile listings, professional directory entries, and authoritative backlinks all signal trust. Google doesn't trust a single source — it trusts consistency across multiple sources.

**3. Content Structure.** Is your content easy for AI to extract? Clear headings, concise definitions, and structured data make it easier for AI Overviews to pull your information. Content buried in marketing copy doesn't get cited.

**4. Freshness and Accuracy.** Is your information current? Google prioritizes content that's recently updated and factually accurate.

### Healthcare-Specific Signals

Healthcare has unique evaluation criteria that other industries don't face:

**E-E-A-T Signals (Experience, Expertise, Authoritativeness, Trustworthiness):** Google's quality rater guidelines emphasize E-E-A-T for "Your Money or Your Life" (YMYL) pages — and healthcare is the most strictly evaluated YMYL category.

- **Experience:** Has the author actually performed the procedure or treated the condition?
- **Expertise:** Is the author a credentialed medical professional?
- **Authoritativeness:** Do other authoritative sources reference this practice or author?
- **Trustworthiness:** Is the information accurate, transparent, and backed by evidence?

A blog post about "IVF success rates" written by a reproductive endocrinologist with 15 years of experience will be cited over a generic health article — if the content is structured for extraction.

**Google Business Profile Completeness:** Your GBP is the single most important signal for local AI Overviews. An incomplete GBP (missing services, no photos, unverified listing) is the #1 reason healthcare practices get excluded from AI Overviews for local queries.

### Content That Gets Cited in AI Overviews

- Clear headings that match patient questions: "What Is a Hair Transplant?"
- Direct answers immediately after headings: "A hair transplant is a surgical procedure that moves hair follicles from one area of the body to another."
- Structured data: tables, lists, step-by-step processes
- Specific facts: success rates, recovery timelines, costs, procedure details

Content that doesn't get cited: marketing language ("We're the best!"), vague descriptions, content buried behind images or carousels, pages that require scrolling past multiple sections to find the answer.

### How to Check Your AI Overview Presence

1. Search Google for "[your specialty] in [your city]"
2. Check if an AI Overview appears — if it does, look at which sources it cite
3. Search for specific patient questions your practice answers
4. Ask ChatGPT, Gemini, and Perplexity the same questions
5. Compare: Are you cited in Google's AI Overview? In AI chatbot answers? In both?

If you're not being cited in any of these, your data isn't structured for AI consumption.

## SEO vs. AEO: What's the Difference?

Search Engine Optimization (SEO) and Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) overlap but serve different purposes:

| Factor | SEO | AEO |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **Goal** | Rank in search results | Be cited in AI answers |
| **Mechanism** | Keywords, backlinks, page speed | Structured data, consistency, clarity |
| **Success metric** | Traffic from search | Mentions in AI responses |
| **Content style** | Keyword-rich, long-form | Direct, extractable, well-structured |
| **User behavior** | Click through multiple results | Receive one AI-generated answer |

A healthcare practice can rank #1 on Google and still be invisible to AI if its data isn't structured for AI consumption. **SEO gets you clicks. AEO gets you recommended.**

## How to Know If Your Practice Is Visible to AI

Run this diagnostic: ask an AI tool (ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity) a question a patient would ask about your specialty in your area. For example:

- "Who's the best cardiologist in Dallas?"
- "What dermatologists accept Aetna near me?"
- "Which clinics offer hormone therapy in Austin?"

If your practice isn't mentioned, AI doesn't know you exist — or doesn't trust your data enough to recommend you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does AI search differ from Google for healthcare?

AI search synthesizes answers from multiple sources instead of ranking web pages. When a patient asks an AI a healthcare question, it pulls information from structured data, directories, and websites to generate one direct answer — unlike Google, which shows ten links for the user to browse.

### Why isn't ranking #1 on Google enough anymore?

Because AI search bypasses Google's ranking system entirely. A practice can rank #1 on Google but be invisible to AI if its data isn't structured for AI consumption. AI needs consistent, structured information across multiple sources to recommend a practice.

### What is Answer Engine Optimization (AEO)?

Answer Engine Optimization (AEO) is the practice of structuring your online presence so that AI systems can find, understand, and recommend your practice. It focuses on being the source AI cites when it answers a patient's question, rather than ranking in Google's results.

### How do I check if my practice is visible to AI?

Ask an AI tool (ChatGPT, Gemini, or Perplexity) a question a patient would ask about your specialty in your area. If your practice isn't mentioned, AI either doesn't know you exist or doesn't trust your data enough to recommend you.

### Can I pay to appear in Google AI Overviews?

No. AI Overviews are generated based on content quality, relevance, and authority — not advertising. You cannot buy your way into an AI Overview. The practices that appear are the ones whose data is most relevant, authoritative, and well-structured.

### What healthcare queries trigger AI Overviews most?

AI Overviews appear most frequently for: "best [specialty] near me," "what is [procedure]," "[specialty] vs [specialty]," "how much does [procedure] cost," and "what to expect from [procedure]." If patients are asking these questions about your specialty, AI Overviews are likely appearing.
