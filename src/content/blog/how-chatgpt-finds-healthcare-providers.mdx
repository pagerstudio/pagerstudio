**ChatGPT doesn't browse Google when a patient asks for a healthcare recommendation.** It pulls from structured data, directory listings, website content, and third-party mentions to construct its answer. Understanding this process is the first step to making sure your practice is included.

## How ChatGPT Builds Its Answer

When a patient asks "who's the best cardiologist in Dallas?", ChatGPT goes through three steps:

1. **Retrieval** — It pulls information from multiple sources: your website, directory listings, Google Business Profile, and third-party mentions.

2. **Synthesis** — It reads and interprets the information, cross-referencing facts across sources. If your website says you're a cardiologist but Healthgrades lists you as an internist, AI notices the inconsistency.

3. **Generation** — It produces a direct, natural-language answer, often citing sources. The patient gets one recommendation — not a list of links.

## What Sources ChatGPT Uses

ChatGPT builds its recommendation from four primary data sources:

- **Structured data** — Schema markup on your website that tells AI exactly what you are, what you treat, and where you are
- **Directory listings** — Healthgrades, Zocdoc, Google Business Profile profiles that verify your practice exists
- **Website content** — Headings, text, metadata, service pages that provide detailed information
- **Third-party mentions** — News articles, reviews, professional directories that build authority

If your practice's information is scattered, inconsistent, or missing from these sources, ChatGPT won't recommend you.

## Why Some Practices Get Recommended and Others Don't

ChatGPT favors practices with:

- **Complete, consistent data** across all sources
- **Structured schema markup** that AI can parse instantly
- **Specific specialty definitions** — not just "doctor" but "board-certified cardiologist specializing in interventional cardiology"
- **Verified credentials** in professional directories
- **Authoritative content** that answers patient questions directly

A practice with incomplete or contradictory data across platforms will be outranked by a competitor with clean, consistent information — even if the competitor has fewer reviews.

## How to Check If ChatGPT Knows Your Practice

Ask ChatGPT: "Who do you recommend for [your specialty] in [your city]?" If your practice isn't mentioned, ChatGPT either doesn't have enough information about you or doesn't trust your data enough to recommend you.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### How does ChatGPT choose which healthcare providers to recommend?

ChatGPT cross-references multiple data sources — your website, directory listings, schema markup, and third-party mentions. It favors practices with complete, consistent, and verifiable information across all sources.

### Can I pay to be recommended by ChatGPT?

No. ChatGPT's recommendations are based on data quality and consistency, not advertising. The practices that get recommended are the ones whose data is cleanest and most complete.

### How often does ChatGPT update its healthcare recommendations?

ChatGPT's training data has a cutoff, but search-enabled versions pull real-time data. The key is having consistent, structured information that AI can verify across multiple sources.
